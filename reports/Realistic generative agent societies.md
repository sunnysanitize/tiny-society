# Give Tiny Society a game master and a ledger

Tiny Society's weakest scores (causal coherence about 6.5, event grounding about 6.5, report faithfulness about 6.3) and most of its observed failures share one structural cause. **Nothing in the engine owns the truth.** No component decides what an attempted action actually achieved, records who now knows which fact, or carries a thread forward until it resolves. The best-documented fix is Concordia's Game Master pattern. Characters only *propose* actions. A separate pass resolves each attempt into a definite, concrete outcome, writes it to a canonical event log, and routes it only to the characters who would plausibly be aware of it ([Concordia repo](https://github.com/google-deepmind/concordia/tree/main/concordia/components/game_master)). Add three more pieces to that pattern: engine-owned progress clocks so multi-day efforts must resolve, a per-character "who knows what" fact ledger with a probabilistic gossip layer so confessions can spread, and a report built only from the cited event log. Together these target five of the seven observed failures. The rest of the literature points the same way. Measured gains in generative-agent work come from explicit state, memory consolidation and per-agent information separation, not from better retrieval ranking. That matches the project's null embedding result. Prompt adjectives such as "be more hostile" or "disagree more" fail in controlled tests ([arXiv 2509.03736](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03736v2)). Evaluation also needs to change before any of this can be judged. Pointwise 1–10 scores that cluster around 7, with 16 runs per arm, can only detect differences of about 0.5 points. Pairwise judging plus countable checks against the event log can detect much smaller effects for less money. Be clear about how strong the evidence is. The architectural direction is well supported, but **no published study measures how much a game master or drama manager improves coherence in a small multi-agent social simulation**. Most specific parameters below are design inferences from social science, not fitted constants.

## The judge's three weakest scores trace to an engine that never adjudicates

The reference architecture is still Park et al.'s Generative Agents. In its ablation, every component added believability. Human raters ranked interview responses, and TrueSkill ratings were fit to the rankings. The full architecture scored **μ = 29.89**. Removing reflection dropped it to 26.88, removing reflection and planning to 25.64, and removing observation, reflection and planning to 21.21, which was below human crowdworkers role-playing the same agents (22.95) ([arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)). Tiny Society already has the two components that ablated most strongly, reflection and planning. The ablation never varied the retrieval weights or the embedding model, so it gives no support for expecting gains from better relevance scoring. The project's 7.21 vs 7.24 embedding result is consistent with that.

What matters more for Tiny Society is that Park's field deployment showed the same failures this project sees. Memories existed but were not retrieved: Rajiv said he hadn't followed the election despite having heard of Sam's candidacy. Agents embellished: Isabella claimed Sam "is going to make an announcement tomorrow," which was never discussed. Information spread slowly. With 25 agents over two game days, knowledge of Sam's candidacy went from **4% to 32%** of the town and knowledge of the Valentine's party from **4% to 52%** ([arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)). If information spreads only when an LLM *chooses* to repeat it, a 5–7 person cast with a handful of exchanges per day will look under-connected. That is the "a confession never reaches anyone else" failure. The paper offered no architectural fix for embellishment.

The most important negative result for this project comes from Zhou et al. They compared a "Script" mode, where one omniscient LLM writes every character, with an "Agents" mode, where each character is a separate LLM with private information. Script mode inflated goal success (**8.44 vs 6.95**) and Craigslist deal rates (**94% vs 30%**). It also let characters "guess" hidden information almost at once, and it made characters overly agreeable. A Mindreaders control, in which separate agents were given each other's private information, behaved close to Script mode. That isolates information leakage as the cause ([arXiv 2403.05020](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.05020v4)). Two implications follow for Tiny Society. First, any prompt that holds several characters' private knowledge will leak it into behavior and make the story look smoother. That applies to a combined multi-turn exchange prompt, the vignette writer, or a planner that sees the global feed. Second, "characters acting on facts they couldn't know" is a known and measurable failure, not random noise.

Concordia's Game Master is the most concrete engineering answer. Players write free-text "putative events." The GM runs a configurable chain of reasoning steps that turns each attempt into a definite event, updates world-state components, and then asks: "Which entities are aware of the event? Answer with a comma-separated list of entity names." Each named player gets the event in its observation queue ([event_resolution.py](https://github.com/google-deepmind/concordia/blob/main/concordia/components/game_master/event_resolution.py)). Several of the prompts in that file map directly onto Tiny Society's failures:

- **`determine_success_and_why`** starts from the default that "if the attempted action is easy to accomplish then the attempt should be successful unless there is a specific reason for it to fail."
- **`attempt_to_most_likely_outcome`** demands concreteness: "it is wrong to say 'Alex finds something'. Instead specify exactly what Alex finds."
- **`result_to_causal_statement`** rewrites the outcome as one cause-and-effect sentence with no hedging.
- **`AccountForAgencyOfOthers`** asks each affected player's *own* agent whether it would really take the voluntary action the event implies. If the answer is no, it re-resolves the event, so one agent's action cannot puppeteer another.
- **`maybe_inject_narrative_push`** asks whether recent events are repetitive. If they are, it adds a complication driven by a non-player character or the world, never by a player character's forced choice.

([Concordia repo](https://github.com/google-deepmind/concordia/tree/main/concordia/components/game_master))

The 2023 paper adds "grounded variables" that the GM checks before accepting an action, such as stopping an agent from spending money it lacks ([arXiv 2312.03664](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.03664)). The honest caveat is that **neither Concordia paper reports an ablation of the resolution chain**. The 2025 paper presents it as an engine design, with the NeurIPS contest as its validation ([arXiv 2507.08892](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08892v1)).

The closest measured evidence for "explicit state beats prose in context" comes from single-player LLM game masters. In a within-subjects study (n=12), a version that added an Archivist agent writing character and location changes to a database scored higher on all 14 constructs than a single-prompt GM. The significant gains included coherent story (p=0.040) and immersion (p=0.034) ([arXiv 2502.19519](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19519v2)). RPGBench separately found that LLMs write engaging stories but "often struggle to implement consistent, verifiable game mechanics" ([arXiv 2502.00595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00595)). Other successor architectures point the same way:

- **Lyfe Agents.** Removing either the asynchronous self-monitoring summary or Summarize-and-Forget memory consolidation "dramatically" lowered murder-mystery success at 3, 6 and 9 agents ([arXiv 2310.02172](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02172)).
- **Humanoid Agents.** Explicit needs and emotion variables verifiably moved behavior. Zeroing a need raised time spent on activities that restore it: health +156%, energy +56% ([arXiv 2310.05418](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05418)).
- **PIANO.** A cognitive controller broadcasts one decision to both the talk and act modules, which keeps speech and action aligned. Its social-awareness modules roughly doubled the slope of perceived vs. true likeability, from 0.175 to 0.365 ([arXiv 2411.00114](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114)).

The common thread is that **ground truth moves out of the agent's context window and into engine-owned structure**, and agents see only what reaches them.

## Measure with counts and paired comparisons, because 1–10 scores cannot see the effects that matter

Before building anything, fix the measuring instrument. The current evaluation has a statistical-power problem. Scores cluster at 6–8, with run-to-run noise of roughly ±0.5. With a run-level SD of σ ≈ 0.5, detecting a 0.25-point improvement at α = 0.05 and 80% power needs about **63 runs per arm** unpaired. Detecting 0.5 points needs about 16 per arm (standard sample-size formula, applied following [Miller, arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)). So the 16-vs-16 embedding A/B could only have detected an effect of about half a point. Its null result means "no large effect," not "no effect." Pairing on world, character set and seed shrinks the variance of the difference by a factor of (1−ρ). At a within-world correlation of ρ = 0.5 the required sample halves, and at ρ = 0.75 it quarters. Miller recommends analysing paired differences, using clustered standard errors when observations are grouped, and doing a power analysis before running ([arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)). The field mostly ignores this. Larooij & Törnberg's review of 35 LLM agent-based-model papers found **~43% validated only subjectively**, none ran sensitivity analyses, and most relied on a single run ([arXiv 2504.03274](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03274v1)).

The judge itself is a second source of noise. In Sotopia-Eval, GPT-4's agreement with humans ranged from **r = 0.71** on goal completion down to **0.45** on believability, **0.33** on knowledge and **0.22** on secret-keeping. The judge was also more lenient than humans on norm and secret violations ([arXiv 2310.11667](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11667v2)). Tiny Society's "character consistency" and "relationship plausibility" dimensions resemble Sotopia's weak dimensions and are probably its noisiest. Chhun et al. found LLM story judges correlate only moderately to weakly with humans on individual stories but very highly at the system level ([arXiv 2405.13769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13769)). That is good news: comparing *configurations* across many runs is what this project needs. Three judge biases are documented:

- **Position bias.** Mitigate it by judging each pair in both orders and counting a win only when both orders agree ([arXiv 2306.05685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)).
- **Self-preference.** A model rates its own outputs higher, and the effect scales with how well it recognizes its own text ([arXiv 2404.13076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076v1)). The current Sonnet-judges-Gemini setup is already cross-family. That protection disappears if a Claude model ever writes the report or narration.
- **Bias toward omniscience.** In TimeChara, the LLM judge penalized characters who correctly said they didn't know something and preferred omniscient, verbose portrayals ([TimeChara](https://ahnjaewoo.github.io/timechara/)). That bias works directly against the "acting on facts they couldn't know" fix. The judge rubric should explicitly reward appropriate ignorance.

Splitting criteria into Boolean checklist items raised agreement across 12 evaluator models and reduced score variance ([CheckEval, arXiv 2403.18771](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18771v2)). Averaging over balanced permutations of the rubric order exposes and reduces rubric position bias ([arXiv 2602.02219](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02219v1)).

The biggest lever is that Tiny Society, unlike most story generators, *has ground truth*: an event log and a per-character record of what each character perceived. That lets most "judge opinion" turn into checking claims against the record. Each check yields hundreds of binary observations per run rather than one 1–10 score:

| Metric | Method | Targets judge dimension | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Report claim precision and recall | Split the report into atomic claims; check each against the event log; also measure the share of key events mentioned | Report faithfulness | FActScore atomic-fact checking ([arXiv 2305.14251](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14251)); recall is an added extension |
| Knowledge-leak rate per 100 actions | Extract the facts each utterance relies on; check them against that speaker's perception log | Event grounding, causal coherence | Design inference from Zhou et al. ([arXiv 2403.05020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05020)) and TimeChara |
| Memory recall probes | Ask each character 3–5 end-of-day questions whose answers are in its own log | Memory / consistency | Automated version of Park's interviews ([arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)) |
| Persona-to-action consistency | An LLM or NLI check of each action against the persona; prompt-to-line discriminates better than line-to-line | Character consistency | Abdulhai et al. ([arXiv 2511.00222](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00222v1)) |
| Diffusion curve | Share of the cast who know each seeded secret, by day; flag 0% and instant 100% | Progression, realism | Park's 4%→52% measure ([arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)) |
| Thread resolution | Share of open threads or clocks resolved by day 7; mean days an effort stays unresolved | Progression | Design inference |
| Repetition and convergence | Near-duplicate action rate (embedding cosine; reuse `embeddings.py`); share of actions aimed at the modal target | Progression, realism | Design inference |
| Conflict and agreeableness rate | Share of interactions with refusal or disagreement; flag monotonic warming | Realism | Over-agreeableness in omniscient sims ([arXiv 2403.05020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05020)) |

Keep the 7-dimension judge as an outer check, but change its protocol. Compare two stories from the same world (baseline vs. treatment) side by side in both orders, then fit a Bradley–Terry model over the wins or run a sign test. Park's believability ablation used exactly this ranking design. Run an A/A test (the same configuration twice per world) once to measure the noise floor and ρ. Judge each story about 3 times to separate judge variance from simulation variance, so you know whether to spend on more judging or more runs. A rough guide: a treatment with a true **70% win rate needs about 45 non-tied pairs** to detect at 80% power, and a 60% win rate needs about 200 (binomial arithmetic, not a sourced figure). Fix seeds and run deterministic or temperature-0 settings for non-agent components such as event casting and the world interpreter. That raises ρ, because only the component under test varies.

## Engine-owned state beats prompt adjectives for every observed pathology

The pathology literature explains why prompt fixes have stalled.

**Agreeableness and conflict avoidance.** Across 9 open models, agents with opposing preferences rarely disagreed outright. Prompts designed to counter sycophancy made the most-opposed pairs agree *more* ([arXiv 2509.03736](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03736v2)). When models do play negative characters, fidelity falls with the character's morality. Mean fidelity dropped from 3.21 for paragons to **2.62 for villains**, and models "substitute nuanced malevolence with superficial aggression" ([arXiv 2511.04962](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04962v2)). This explains why some worlds turn out too polite and others turn into manufactured drama: both are the model's prior rather than the world's pressure.

**Echoing.** Agents drop their role and mirror their partner in **5–70%** of agent-to-agent conversations, typically after 7 or more turns. More reasoning effort does not cure it. Structured response protocols cut it to about 9%, while prompt engineering reduced it only partly ([arXiv 2511.09710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09710)). This bears directly on the charged multi-turn exchanges in `engine.py`: keep them short and keep each turn schema-structured.

**Persona collapse.** All 10 models tested on 1,144 personas showed it. Thinking mode didn't help, and role-play fine-tuning made population coverage *worse* ([arXiv 2604.24698](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24698v1)).

**Persona drift.** Over 100+ round dialogues, persona fidelity degrades toward the model's default assistant voice, especially in goal-oriented talk ([arXiv 2512.12775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12775)).

**Generic reasoning in role-play.** Chain-of-thought degraded role-play on 4 of 6 benchmarks across 24 LLMs, through attention diversion and a drift toward a formal reasoning style ([ACL Findings 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.537)).

The mitigations with evidence behind them share one pattern: move state and constraint into the engine and the schema. The table maps each observed failure to its mechanism and fix.

| Observed failure | Likely mechanism | Mitigation | Engine component |

Two cheap techniques sit on top of this.

**A persona-consistency critic with best-of-2 resampling.** Score each candidate action for prompt-to-line consistency, knowledge validity and novelty. This is an inference-time version of the consistency reward that cut inconsistency by **more than 55%** when used for RL training ([arXiv 2511.00222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00222v1)). No paper measures the inference-time version directly. Given the budget, apply it only when a cheap deterministic check (an unknown-fact reference, a near-duplicate action, an out-of-profile intent) flags the action.

**A daily self-summary per character.** This is Lyfe's strongest ablation. Have `reflector.py` write a short running narrative every day ("who I am, what I'm trying to do, what changed today, what I'm waiting on") instead of a reflection every four days. Always put it in the prompt, along with per-relationship summaries and open commitments. With 5–7 agents over 7 days, consolidated memory fits in context. **Always-included structured summaries are likely to matter more than retrieval ranking.** Park's documented failures were retrieval *misses*, and structured state cannot be missed.

## Social science supplies the dynamics the LLM should not be asked to invent

The deterministic layer in `deterministic.py`, `consequence.py` and `observation.py` should carry dynamics that the LLM performs poorly or inconsistently. Humanoid Agents found LLMs predicted physical needs well (fullness F1 0.972) but fun (0.66) and social states poorly, mostly by over-predicting them ([arXiv 2310.05418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05418v1)). Unless noted, the parameters below are design values informed by the literature, not fitted constants. Sources marked † in the research notes were cited from background knowledge and should be spot-checked before they appear in user-facing text.

### Gossip should be the default, with confessions leaking at a modest, trait-dependent rate

Social topics take about **two-thirds of casual conversation time** in Dunbar's 1997 coding of 45 conversations. A 2025 replication put it at about 85% ([Szala et al. 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/61BF5C91583F04053BE9471ED96FAE1D/S1866980824000541a.pdf/how-much-conversation-content-is-actually-social-human-conversational-behaviour-revisited.pdf)). A realistic village or crew therefore talks about absent members constantly. In Tiny Society, information currently moves only when the LLM decides to repeat it. Classic rumor models give a simple mechanism. In Maki–Thompson, a spreader who tells someone who already knows becomes a "stifler," and in the large-population limit about **20% of people never hear the rumor** ([arXiv 2002.06821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.06821)). Leaving some people out of the loop is a feature, and it is good story fuel. Retelling distorts content, though less than the famous lab results suggest. Allport & Postman described **leveling** (the story gets shorter), **sharpening** (a few details get exaggerated) and **assimilation** (the story bends toward the teller's biases). Field studies show far less distortion than their lab chains ([Wikipedia: Rumor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumor)).

The concrete design is a `KnowledgeItem` per fact, with fields `{id, about, valence, arousal, secrecy, source, fidelity, hops}`, held per character. When A interacts with B and holds fact f about a third person, A retells it with probability `base(0.3–0.5) × novelty × juiciness × trust(A→B) × (1 − secrecy_penalty) × norm_bonus`. Use secrecy penalties of 0, 0.5 and 0.85 for public, private and explicitly secret facts. Raise the probability when the subject broke a group norm, in line with the norm-enforcement view of gossip ([Kniffin & Wilson 2005](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/860457736)). Apply the Maki–Thompson stifler rule. Have the LLM reword each retelling under constraints set by code: drop a detail, push valence away from neutral by 10–20%, and tilt toward the teller's affinity for the subject.

A confession creates a `secret` item. It leaks at roughly 0.15 × the other modifiers per opportunity, faster if the confidant's affinity toward the confessor falls or the confidant is low on Agreeableness or HEXACO Honesty-Humility. When a subject learns they were gossiped about, apply an amplified betrayal bid toward the teller. That one mechanism fixes "the confession never reaches anyone" and supplies mid-week reveals. The 0.15 leak rate and the ally/rival multipliers are design guesses. No empirical per-dyad transmission rate for small face-to-face groups was found.

### Emotion should decay on per-emotion clocks, not be overwritten

Real emotion durations span orders of magnitude. In Verduyn & Lavrijsen's study of 27 emotions, **shame averaged about 30 minutes and sadness about 120 hours**, with rumination and event importance driving duration ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/10/141030133119.htm)). Affect also has inertia: current affect is predicted by prior affect, and inertia is higher with higher neuroticism ([Kuppens et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2901421)). Overwriting `mood` with each action's `emotional_reaction`, as `applicator.py` does now, guarantees whiplash.

The ALMA architecture gives a ready-made three-layer design. **Emotions** are short-lived OCC-typed appraisal outputs. **Mood** is a PAD (pleasure, arousal, dominance) vector that active emotions pull on and that decays toward a personality-derived baseline. **Personality** is fixed and sets the baseline and the decay rates ([ALMA](https://alma.dfki.de/); [paper](https://cs.huji.ac.il/course/2005/aisemin/articles2006/docs/pa1a3_29.pdf)). Gebhard & Kipp reported that users found ALMA's generated emotions and moods plausible ([Springer](https://www.springerprofessional.de/are-computer-generated-emotions-and-moods-plausible-to-humans/1232988)).

In Tiny Society, `perception.py` should ask the receiving character's LLM for appraisal variables: desirability for self, praiseworthiness of the actor's act, and target. Code then maps those to an emotion and an intensity. Each emotion decays with a half-life by type: about 0.5–2 hours for shame, surprise or irritation, 6–12 hours for anger or pride, 1–3 days for grief. Rumination lengthens the half-life for high-neuroticism characters. The mood update is `mood ← baseline + ρ(mood − baseline) + κΣ(emotion PAD × intensity)`, with ρ ≈ 0.6–0.8 per half-day and two or three extra decay steps overnight. The LLM sees a *computed* label. Keep emotional contagion tiny, a nudge of about 0.05 toward co-present peers, because measured contagion effects are small: the Facebook feed experiment found d ≈ 0.001–0.02 (†, [doi:10.1073/pnas.1320040111](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1320040111)).

### Relationships need a slow closeness axis, asymmetric negativity and a conflict ladder

Friendship is accumulated time, not a series of bids. Hall's studies estimate **about 40–60 hours to casual friendship, 80–100 to friendship and over 200 to close friendship**, with leisure and talk counting far more than working side by side ([KU News](https://news.ku.edu/2018/03/06/study-reveals-number-hours-it-takes-make-friend)). At realistic contact levels, seven days supports "casual friend → friend" at most. Fast bonds therefore need a stated cause, such as reciprocated self-disclosure, a shared crisis or the world premise. Closeness to friends is more sensitive to reduced contact than closeness to kin ([Roberts & Dunbar 2011](https://www.neuroscience.ox.ac.uk/publications/572905)). Negative interactions weigh more than positive ones. Gottman's stable couples run about **5:1 positive to negative during conflict**, against about 0.8:1 for couples headed for divorce ([Gottman Institute](https://gottman.com/blog/the-magic-relationship-ratio-according-science/)).

`consequence.py` should change in five ways:

1. Split a slow `closeness` axis, built from weighted `hours_together` with a world-specific time-compression factor, from the fast signed `affinity` axis.
2. Scale negative bids 2–5× relative to positive ones.
3. Decay closeness only on days without contact, and let affinity drift back toward the level closeness implies rather than toward 0. The current flat 0.01/day decay is too coarse.
4. Gate romance on a closeness tier plus a reciprocated disclosure.
5. Add a `conflict_level` from 0 to 4: tension → open disagreement → hostile acts → coalition-seeking (which triggers gossip about the opponent) → rupture. Leaving the ladder requires an apology the other party *accepts*, with acceptance probability rising with Agreeableness and closeness.

Once a day, a Heider balance pass should nudge the weakest edge of each strongly unbalanced triad by 0.02–0.05. It should also tell the LLM about the imbalance as a felt tension, for example "Mara likes Jon, whom you distrust" (†, [Heider 1946](https://doi.org/10.1080/00223980.1946.9917275)). Most importantly, affinity should update from the *resolved* event the adjudicator produces, not from the intent verb the actor declared. An insult that the adjudicator rules was never overheard should not damage the relationship.

### Opinions should form factions, not collapse to the group mean

The current stance-conformity drip in `deterministic.py` is close to DeGroot averaging, which converges to consensus on any connected trust graph (†, [DeGroot 1974](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1974.10480137)). That is unrealistic for a week of conflict. Bounded-confidence models keep stable factions instead. In Deffuant–Weisbuch, two agents move toward each other only if their opinions differ by less than d, and the number of surviving clusters is about **⌊1/(2d)⌋** ([Weisbuch et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0111494)). Friedkin–Johnsen adds stubbornness by anchoring each agent to its initial opinion (†, [doi:10.1080/0022250X.1990.9990069](https://doi.org/10.1080/0022250X.1990.9990069)). Asch's classic results show about 37% conformity on critical trials, cut sharply by a single dissenting ally (†, [Asch 1956](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0093718)).

The proposed update has three parts:

- **Pairwise bounded confidence.** Update a stance only when the topic actually came up between two characters: `Δx_i = μ·w_ij·(x_j − x_i)` if `|x_j − x_i| < d_i`, with `d_i = 0.2 + 0.3·Openness`, μ ≈ 0.1–0.2, and w weighted by affinity and status. Hostile pairs should push apart.
- **Stubbornness.** Anchor core-value stances with λ ≈ 0.5–0.8, and raise λ after a character states a stance publicly.
- **Public vs. private stance.** Compute the stance a character expresses in a group scene as its private stance pulled toward a unanimous majority with probability about 0.3–0.4, dropping to about 0.1 if anyone else shares the dissent. The gap between what someone says in the meeting and what they say in private is itself gossip material.

### Needs and personality should parameterize the engine, not just decorate the prompt

Needs give characters off-screen reasons to break routine, which counters convergence. The Sims scores actions with **nonlinear urgency curves**: a need is ignorable until it runs low, then dominates. It also picks randomly among the top options rather than always taking the single best ([GMTK](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/gmtk-the-genius-ai-behind-the-sims)). Real people socialize far less than story characters do. US adults average **34 minutes a day** of socializing and communicating, about 2 hours on days they socialize at all ([BLS ATUS 2023](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/atus_06272024.htm)).

A light version for Tiny Society:

- **Needs.** Track 4–6 needs (energy, social, solitude/recovery, purpose, plus 0–2 world-specific ones such as faith or oxygen) with `urgency = w(1 − level)^k`, k ≈ 2–3. Pass only the top one or two to the LLM as "what you feel like doing," and credit restoration in code.
- **Desire profiles.** Give each character 3–4 stable "desire" emphases drawn from Reiss's list (†), such as status, acceptance or vengeance, mapped to the intent verbs they reward. This pushes characters toward *different* targets.
- **Off-screen routine.** Treat 60–80% of each day as routine that the narrator summarizes.

On personality, within-person variability in momentary trait expression is as large as the variability between people, while each person's average stays very stable (†, [Fleeson 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.80.6.1011)). Realistic consistency therefore means a stable *distribution* of behavior per character, not a prototypical act every turn.

Compute a trait → parameter table once per character. Extraversion sets social-need decay and initiation probability. Agreeableness damps negative bids and raises apology acceptance. Neuroticism lowers the mood baseline and raises emotion half-life and inertia. Openness sets the bounded-confidence radius. Conscientiousness sets secret-keeping. Honesty-Humility lowers the leak and manipulation rates. Mehrabian's regressions give the PAD baseline from the Big Five (†; verify the sign convention, since N is coded as stability) ([ALMA paper](https://cs.huji.ac.il/course/2005/aisemin/articles2006/docs/pa1a3_29.pdf)). If a character's rolling intent mix drifts far from its traits for more than a day, add one corrective "you're usually…" line to the prompt.

## Clocks, fronts and a threads ledger make stories progress and the report traceable

The "repetitive multi-day efforts with no outcome" failure has a tabletop solution with decades of play-testing behind it. **Progress clocks** from Blades in the Dark size an obstacle as 4, 6 or 8 segments by difficulty, and a long-term project as 8 segments. Danger clocks tick 1–3 segments per consequence and fire when full. There are also racing clocks (two clocks in opposition), linked clocks (filling one unlocks the next), countdown clocks and faction clocks ([Roll20 BitD SRD](https://roll20.net/compendium/BITD/Rules:Progress%20clocks)). Apocalypse World's **fronts** advance through "grim portents" whether or not the player characters engage, and player action can redirect or halt them ([Sly Flourish](https://slyflourish.com/fronts_in_dnd.html)). RimWorld's storyteller shows how to pace this. It watches the colony's state and schedules incidents along a rising and falling tension curve, with breathing room after spikes. Its designer settles every conflict between "game" and "story generator" in favor of the story generator ([PC Gamer via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/pc-gamer-us/20170523/283794263693348); [RimWorld Wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/AI_storyteller)).

Applied to Tiny Society, the per-day loop gets an **Adjudicator** between `reasoner.py` and `applicator.py`. It runs only for actions classified as *attempts*: search, investigate, persuade, steal, hide, repair, confront-with-demand. Everything else applies as now, which keeps cost bounded. The adjudicator works in four steps:

1. One structured Gemini call labels the difficulty and the actor's relevant competence (0–2) from the persona and the world.
2. Code rolls a seeded 2d6 in Powered-by-the-Apocalypse style: 10+ success, 7–9 success at a cost, 6 or less failure with a complication. (The PbtA bands were not source-verified in this research.)
3. The same call, or a second one, writes the concrete result for that band. It is bound by Concordia's rules: name exactly what is found, and never decide another character's voluntary action. It returns `{outcome, concrete_result, caused_by:[event_ids], clock_ticks, state_deltas, observers, reveals_fact_id}`.
4. Code applies the deltas to the world state and the event log.

Putting the roll in code makes outcomes reproducible under a seed, which also raises ρ in paired evaluation. It also takes the "does it succeed?" decision away from a model that otherwise tends to say yes to everything or stall indefinitely.

Every multi-day effort becomes an engine-owned clock. "Find who took the ledger" is an 8-segment clock that gains 0, 1, 2 or 3 ticks per attempt depending on the outcome band, and it *must* resolve when full. At one or two attempts a day, that is roughly 3–5 days, which suits a 7-day run. `event_casting.py` already produces a hidden truth and per-character private involvement. Each hidden-truth fact should get a `reveal_condition`, such as "investigation clock reaches 4/8" or "succeeds at searching the mill." When the condition fires, the adjudicator puts the fact into `concrete_result`, and only the observers receive it. That joins the hidden truth to the gossip layer and makes event grounding structural rather than hoped-for.

The 5-day dynamic event should become a **front**: a danger clock that ticks daily whatever the cast does, racing against their response clock. Beyond that, a cheap **daily director** call should replace one-shot dynamic events. It reads a target tension curve (for example 2, 3, 5, 4, 7, 9, 5 across days 1–7), scores current tension from state (clocks near full, conflict levels, open accusations), and either injects one complication or withholds one. Following Concordia's `maybe_inject_narrative_push`, a complication may come from a non-player character or the world but never forces a player character's choice. Following Façade's beat selection, the director picks beats whose preconditions hold and whose declared effects best close the gap to the target tension ([Mateas & Stern 2003](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~michaelm/tenurereview/publications/mateas-tidse2003.pdf)). From day 5 onward the director stops opening threads and only advances or closes existing ones. Every clock gets a hard deadline at day 7.

A **threads ledger** (a "Chekhov ledger") tracks every setup with a status of open, advancing, resolved or abandoned. Setups include the event's hidden truth, each private involvement, accusations, promises and introduced objects. The director favors stale open threads. Recent LLM experience-manager work found that "no manager dominates on every property" across player agency, controllability, author intent and robustness ([arXiv 2611.02807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02807)). Treat intervention strength as a tunable dial rather than a single setting.

Faithful narration comes last, and it is where James Ryan's "curationist" view earns its place. Simulations become stories only once someone selects and frames events ([Ryan 2018](https://escholarship.org/content/qt1340j5h2/qt1340j5h2.pdf?t=plxqme)). Story sifting tools give the method:

- **Felt** matches hand-written patterns over an event log ([Felt](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Felt_SimpleStorySifter)).
- **Winnow** matches patterns incrementally while the simulation runs and flags *partial* matches, sequences that have started but not finished ([AIIDE 2021](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18903)).
- **"Select the Unexpected"** ranks sifted results by how surprising they are ([ICIDS 2022](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/StU_ICIDS2022.pdf)).

A handful of patterns over Tiny Society's log would give the director arcs to complete during the run. Examples: "secret reaches the person it concerns," "effort fails twice then succeeds," "A turns on B after B helped A," "rivalry escalates over three or more events."

`reporter.py` should then work in five steps:

1. Sift and rank 3–5 throughlines from pattern matches and thread payoffs.
2. Give the narrator *only* the chosen event records plus the threads ledger. Do not give it raw agent memories, plans or explanations, which carry the Zhou-style omniscience risk.
3. Require an `[E123]`-style citation after every factual sentence.
4. Check in code that every cited event exists and that the sentence's actor took part in it. Optionally add a cheap entailment check per sentence.
5. Strip the citations for display, and name every unresolved thread explicitly rather than dropping it silently.

No published study measures citation-constrained narration of simulation logs. The design follows standard practice for attribution in retrieval-augmented generation, but it is also the most directly checkable change on this list, because the FActScore-style claim-precision metric measures it.

## A prioritized roadmap, ordered by expected impact per dollar

The ordering follows two principles. Build the measuring instrument first. Then build the components that create ground truth (the adjudicator, ledger and event log), because almost everything else reads from them. The cost column counts extra LLM calls per run on Gemini 2.5 Flash. Assuming about 6 agents × 7 days with roughly one action each per day, plus exchanges, an extra call per attempt-type action adds tens of calls, not hundreds. That should fit within the existing ~$0.10–0.15 envelope if the adjudicator is limited to attempts and merged into a single structured call. Confirm this with real token counts.

Items 1 through 3 should be built together if possible, because the adjudicator writes the log that the report and the leak checker read. Deprioritize further embedding work. Neither the literature nor the project's A/B supports it, and the memory gains that are measured come from summarization, deduplication and always-in-context structure. Fine-tuning is not recommended. Role-play fine-tunes can *reduce* persona diversity: CoSER-Llama-8B covered only 0.16 of human archetypes, against 0.80 for Qwen3-4B ([arXiv 2604.24698](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24698v1)).

### Where the evidence is strong and where this report is designing

The evidence is strong for the following:

- Separating each agent's information matters, and omniscient context inflates smoothness and agreement (Zhou et al.).
- Reflection, planning and memory consolidation measurably improve believability (Park; Lyfe).
- Prompt-only fixes fail for agreeableness and echoing, while structured protocols help.
- LLM judges are biased by position and self-preference, and reliable mainly at the system level.
- Paired designs and many binary sub-measures increase statistical power.
- The social phenomena themselves: gossip prevalence, negativity asymmetry, friendship timescales, emotion duration differences, and bounded-confidence faction formation.

The evidence is moderate for structured world state improving perceived coherence. That rests on one n=12 single-player study and benchmark observations.

The following are design inferences without direct measurement in multi-agent social simulations:

- Concordia's resolution chain, progress clocks and drama-management tension curves.
- Citation-constrained narration.
- The knowledge-leak metric.
- Inference-time best-of-n with a persona critic.
- Every numeric parameter for gossip, mood, closeness, opinion and needs.

Several supporting papers were read only as abstracts or snippets: echoing, persona drift, CriticaLLM, the HAMLET and Triangle frameworks, and split-softmax. Their effect sizes should be treated as indicative. No source covered Gemini 2.5 Flash's role-play behavior specifically.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what "realism" means for Tiny Society. It is less a property of how well each character *talks* than of whether the world *keeps score*. Every published system that measurably improved coherence did it by taking something away from the LLM: the authority to decide outcomes, the knowledge it shouldn't have, or the freedom to rewrite its own mood each turn. It then handed that thing to code or a separate adjudicating pass. The project's own data already hint at this. Embeddings, a pure retrieval improvement, changed nothing. The failures that remain are failures of bookkeeping (who knows what, what came of the search, what the report may claim), not failures of eloquence.

The less obvious implication concerns evaluation and the build order. The canonical event log is more than the fix for report faithfulness. It is the ground truth that makes cheap, high-power evaluation possible, because leaks, diffusion, thread resolution and claim support can all be counted against it. Building the adjudicator and the log first therefore improves the simulation and makes the remaining roadmap testable at the same time. That matters, because the most valuable step after it, calibrating the design-guess parameters for gossip, mood and closeness, can only be done empirically, and only with an instrument sensitive enough to see quarter-point effects.
